Lambert Lake, and the family it's named for

Three-quarters of a mile east of our house on Woodview Court there used to be a lake shaped like a ring, with an island in the middle. The maps called it Lambert Lake and Lambert Island. It was drained in the 1940s, and today it's a wetland that most people drive past on I-35E without knowing it was ever there.

The Lambert it's named for is family. Abraham Lambert, Eric's fourth-great-grandfather, bought that island from the United States government in 1849.

From Quebec to Little Canada

Abraham Lambert was born on 5 June 1792 at Lauzon, across the St. Lawrence from Quebec City, the son of Louis Joseph Lambert and Judith Magnon. On 29 December 1821, at Lévis, he married Rose Angèle Demers. They had at least nine children.

In 1841, when Abraham was nearly fifty, the family left Lower Canada. The county history of 1881 tells it this way:

Abram Lambert with his family of wife and three sons and three daughters left Lower Canada, in 1841…

They traveled by way of St. Louis, headed for Oregon. They stopped instead at St. Boniface on the Red River, where their daughter Rose Angélique married Alexander Ducharme in November 1844. The next year both families came south together and settled at Little Canada, among the French-Canadian families around Lake Gervais.

25 March 1849

On a single day the federal land office at the Falls of St. Croix issued Abraham two land patents. One was 91 acres on the west shore of Lake Gervais, the family farm. The other was the whole island in the middle of the lake that already carried his name: lots 6 and 7 of section 29, 94.39 acres.

Those two lots are exactly the island. On the government's original survey plat, lot 6 is 58.69 acres and lot 7 is 35.70; together that's 94.39, the patent's figure to the hundredth of an acre.

A founder of Little Canada

In 1850 Abraham was an election judge for the Little Canada precinct. Around then he was named its postmaster, at what an 1857 newspaper called the first post office in Minnesota outside St. Paul. He gave the land for St. John's cemetery in 1851, and the timbers for the first log church there came mostly from Lambert land.

He was also, in the words of a 1964 county history article, "the stubborn Abraham Lambert": when the parish started charging rent for pews, he carried his own folding chair to Mass instead. He died at Little Canada on 30 August 1875, at 83, and is buried in the family lot at St. John's. Rose Angèle died the next year.

How long the family held the land

1849Abraham buys the island and the Lake Gervais farm from the government.
1853His son Louis A. Lambert buys the 80 acres next to the farm.
1867The county map shows Lamberts across the west side of Lake Gervais: "P. & J. M. Lambert" (likely sons Pierre and Joseph Modeste), "Louis Lambert", about 100–150 acres. The island is already someone else's (P. Jervais).
1874–86Pierre Lambert holds 63 acres with a house; Louis still has the shore lots.
1898The west shore of Lake Gervais is platted as "Gervais Lake Park". No Lamberts left on the maps there.
1931The Ducharmes, Rose Angélique's family, are still there, along what is still called Ducharme Road.

The island left the family within twenty years, but the name stayed on the lake. The 1881 county history says the lake was "named in honor of Louis Lambert, who purchased a portion of the island located near its center in an early day." Louis was Abraham's son, and our direct ancestor.

What happened to the lake

The 1902 government topographic map already draws marsh where the ring of water was. In the 1940s County Ditch 14, the channel now called Lambert Creek, drained what was left, for a development that was later abandoned. Lambert Creek still runs from the old lakebed into East Vadnais Lake, St. Paul's drinking-water reservoir. A pond was dug inside the old lake in 2005, and in the winter of 2020–21 the straightened ditch was given its bends back.
